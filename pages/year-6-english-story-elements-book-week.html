About This Year 6 Theme & Plot Worksheet

This Year 6 English resource asks students to make a genuine comparison between two literary texts, rather than writing two separate summaries.

Students identify a shared theme, compare the challenges and choices faced by characters, examine important plot events and use evidence from both texts to justify their conclusions.

It is part of the My Story Symphony – Book Week 2026 collection from Aussie Primary Academy.

Learning Intention

“I can compare how two literary texts develop a similar theme through characters and events.”

Australian Curriculum V9

AC9E6LE02

Supports students to identify similarities and differences in literary texts on similar topics, themes or plots.

Skills Practised

  • Identifying theme
  • Comparing literary texts
  • Comparing plots
  • Comparing character responses
  • Using evidence from two texts
  • Making and justifying judgements

Teacher / Parent Tip

Encourage students to connect both texts within the same response rather than discussing each one separately.

Useful comparison words include both, similarly, however and whereas.

Differentiation

Support: Use a comparison table and sentence frames such as “Both texts show…, but Text A…, whereas Text B…”.

Core: Students identify a shared theme and support a comparison using evidence from both texts.

Extend: Ask students to evaluate which text develops the shared theme more effectively and defend their judgement with evidence from both texts.

Why This Helps Readers

Comparing literary texts develops higher-order comprehension. Students must connect ideas across texts, identify meaningful similarities and differences, select evidence and explain how different stories can explore a related theme in different ways.

How to Use This Worksheet

  • Whole class: Model the difference between summarising two texts and directly comparing them.
  • Guided reading: Identify the shared theme before comparing character choices and plot events.
  • Independent practice: Students complete the comparison using evidence from both texts.
  • Writing connection: Use comparison sentence starters to develop a structured analytical paragraph.
