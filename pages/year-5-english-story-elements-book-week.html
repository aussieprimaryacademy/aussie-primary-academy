About This Year 5 Point of View Worksheet

This Year 5 English resource helps students explore how point of view changes the way a story is understood. Students compare perspectives and consider what each narrator or character knows.

They separate evidence from assumptions and explain how new information can change a reader's judgement of a character or event.

It is part of the My Story Symphony – Book Week 2026 collection from Aussie Primary Academy.

Learning Intention

“I can explain how point of view influences my response to events and characters.”

Australian Curriculum V9

AC9E5LE03

Supports students to recognise that point of view influences how readers interpret and respond to events and characters.

Teacher / Parent Tip

Create two columns: “What the narrator knows” and “What the reader later learns.”

Ask students whether the new information changes their opinion of a character and require them to explain why.

Differentiation

Support: Read the viewpoints separately and highlight exactly what each narrator or character knows.

Core: Students compare the viewpoints and explain how each perspective influences their interpretation.

Extend: Ask students to rewrite part of the scene from a third character's viewpoint and identify which information changes.

Why This Helps Readers

Point-of-view analysis develops critical reading. Students discover that their interpretation of an event or character can change depending on who tells the story, what that person knows and what information is withheld.

How to Use This Worksheet

  • Whole class: Compare how two people might describe the same event differently.
  • Guided reading: Highlight what each viewpoint reveals and what it leaves unknown.
  • Independent practice: Students compare perspectives and justify their interpretation with evidence.
  • Writing connection: Rewrite a short event from another character's perspective.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is point of view?

Point of view is the perspective from which events are presented. Different viewpoints can give readers different information about the same character or event.

Why compare two viewpoints?

Comparing viewpoints helps students notice how access to different information can change interpretation and judgement.
