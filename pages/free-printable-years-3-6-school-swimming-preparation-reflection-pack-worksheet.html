Purpose of this resource

Support preparation and reflection around school swimming sessions, focusing on equipment, safe decisions, communication and next steps.

What’s included

  • Review equipment
  • consider pool-safety scenarios
  • identify when to ask for help
  • reflect on a lesson and an instructor-agreed goal.

How teachers can use it

Use before and after sessions. Align discussion with venue rules, the school’s risk-management plan and instructor guidance. Goals must suit the student’s assessed ability.

Practical classroom approach

  • Explain the purpose and model an example.
  • Allow students to work at a comfortable pace.
  • Use responses to guide conversation, not as high-stakes assessment.

Guidance for parents and carers

Check the school’s program information for equipment and arrangements. Encourage your child to tell the teacher or instructor if unwell, anxious, cold, tired or unsure. Raise support needs early.

Keep it supportive

Focus on listening, encouragement and the child’s own words. There is no need to complete every prompt in one sitting.

Inclusive ways to adapt the activities

Allow written, spoken or diagram responses. Discuss scenarios without demonstrating unsafe behaviour. Reflection is not a formal assessment of water competence.

Helpful adjustments

  • Offer short sessions and one-step instructions.
  • Accept drawing, speaking, pointing or adult scribing where appropriate.
  • Provide extra processing time and breaks.

Important water-safety reminder

This printable supports preparation and reflection only. It does not teach swimming, assess water competence or replace active, attentive adult supervision, qualified swimming instruction, school risk-management procedures or the swimming venue’s safety rules. Children must be supervised by responsible adults whenever they are in or around water.

Helpful information

Frequently asked questions

Is this a substitute for swimming lessons?

No. It is not swimming instruction and does not assess water competence. Follow the school’s program and qualified instructor guidance.

How should learning goals be selected?

Goals should be realistic, appropriate to current ability and agreed with the qualified swimming instructor.
