About this worksheet

This three-page Foundation English worksheet uses a familiar story, poem or picture book to help students notice story features and make personal connections. Students complete a story map covering the title, main character, beginning and ending, then explain how an experience is similar to or different from a character's experience. The final page includes guidance for adults; responses are open-ended rather than fixed.

Learning intention

We are learning to notice story features and connect ideas to our own experiences.

Success looks like: identifying a story's title, character, beginning and ending, then sharing an understandable personal connection.

Curriculum alignment

AC9EFLE01: Share ideas about stories, poems and images in literature, reflecting on experiences that are similar or different to their own.

AC9EFLE03: Recognise different types of literary texts and identify features including events, characters, beginnings and endings.

This worksheet provides focused practice related to these Australian Curriculum v9.0 descriptions; it does not represent full coverage of either description.

Skills practised

  • Identifying a story title and main character.
  • Recalling what happens at the beginning and end.
  • Recognising basic story features.
  • Connecting a character's experience with the student's own experience.
  • Explaining a similarity or difference in words or pictures.

Teacher and parent notes

Choose a familiar, age-appropriate story, poem or picture book before beginning. Students may need the text reread or key events discussed before completing the story map. The answer guide notes that responses will vary; look for a clear story feature and an understandable personal connection rather than one exact answer.

How to use this worksheet

  1. Choose a familiar story, poem or picture book.
  2. On page 1, complete the story map with the title, main character, beginning and ending.
  3. On page 2, complete the personal connection prompts and draw one important connection.
  4. Use page 3 to review the guidance for assessing responses.

Differentiation

Support: Reread the story and offer picture prompts or oral sentence starters such as “This reminds me of…”. Complete the story map together.

Core: Identify the main story features and explain one personal connection.

Extend: Compare two characters' experiences or explain more than one way an event is similar to or different from the student's own experience.

Frequently asked questions

Does the worksheet include a reading passage?
No. Students use a familiar story, poem or picture book they have read or heard.

Are there fixed answers?
No. The final page explains that responses vary and provides guidance on what to look for.

Can students draw their connection?
Yes. Page 2 includes space to draw one important connection.
