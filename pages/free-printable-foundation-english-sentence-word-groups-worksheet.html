About this worksheet

This three-page Foundation English worksheet helps students see how groups of words work together to make a sentence meaningful. Students put mixed-up words in order, write the completed sentences, then build new sentences by choosing a “who”, “does what” and “where” part. The final page includes suggested answers and a teacher/parent guide.

Learning intention

We are learning that groups of words work together to make a sentence meaningful.

Success looks like: arranging words in an order that makes sense and combining a subject, action and place to create a sentence.

Curriculum alignment

AC9EFLA06: Recognise that sentences are made up of groups of words that work together in particular ways to make meaning.

This worksheet provides focused practice related to this Australian Curriculum v9.0 description; it does not represent full coverage of the content description.

Skills practised

  • Recognising that word order affects sentence meaning.
  • Sequencing words to form a meaningful sentence.
  • Identifying who or what a sentence is about.
  • Connecting an action with a person, animal or thing.
  • Adding a where phrase to build a sentence.

Teacher and parent notes

Ask the student to read each completed sentence aloud and check whether it makes sense. If needed, discuss who the sentence is about and what that person or animal does. The answer guide provides suggested sentence orders and accepts other grammatical combinations.

How to use this worksheet

  1. On page 1, number the mixed-up words in order, then write each sentence.
  2. On page 2, choose one option from each “Who?”, “Does what?” and “Where?” group to build two sentences.
  3. Read each sentence aloud and check that it makes sense.
  4. Use page 3 to review the suggested answers and adult guidance.

Differentiation

Support: Cut out or write the word groups on separate cards. Model how to find who the sentence is about before arranging the remaining words.

Core: Complete the word-order tasks and build two sentences using the three choice groups.

Extend: Create additional sentences with a different subject, action or place, then explain how changing a word group changes the meaning.

Frequently asked questions

Are answers included?
Yes. Page 3 includes suggested answers and a teacher/parent guide.

Does the worksheet ask students to write their own sentences?
Yes. Page 2 asks students to create two sentences using the word groups.

What if a student makes a different sentence?
The answer guide says to accept other grammatical combinations that make sense.
