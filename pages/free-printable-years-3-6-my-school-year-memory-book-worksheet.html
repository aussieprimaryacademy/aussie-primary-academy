Purpose of this resource

Help students reflect on experiences and evidence of learning, recognise progress and prepare a constructive goal.

What’s included

  • Capture highlights
  • identify learning progress
  • reflect on a challenge and strategies that helped
  • write to the next teacher
  • choose a future goal.

How teachers can use it

Introduce it as personal reflection, not graded work. Model specific reflection by naming a skill, describing evidence or identifying a helpful strategy. Sharing is optional.

Practical classroom approach

  • Explain the purpose and model an example.
  • Allow students to work at a comfortable pace.
  • Use responses to guide conversation, not as high-stakes assessment.

Guidance for parents and carers

Ask what your child wants to remember and what they feel proud of learning. Encourage specific examples rather than ranking achievements. Let them choose what to share.

Keep it supportive

Focus on listening, encouragement and the child’s own words. There is no need to complete every prompt in one sitting.

Inclusive ways to adapt the activities

Offer planning prompts, word banks, speech-to-text or scribing. Students choose which experiences to record and may leave prompts blank. Do not require private information or sensitive personal or family disclosures.

Helpful adjustments

  • Offer short sessions and one-step instructions.
  • Accept drawing, speaking, pointing or adult scribing where appropriate.
  • Provide extra processing time and breaks.

Helpful information

Frequently asked questions

Is this a formal assessment?

No. It is a reflection and memory resource, not a graded assessment or diagnostic tool.

Can it be used in a class portfolio?

Yes, where students are comfortable and school privacy and portfolio processes are followed.
