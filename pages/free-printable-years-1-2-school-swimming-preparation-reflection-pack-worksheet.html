Purpose of this resource

A preparation and reflection companion for discussion about equipment, pool safety, feelings, support and small learning goals.

What’s included

  • Check what to bring
  • discuss pool-safety choices
  • identify ways to ask an adult for support
  • reflect on a lesson and a manageable next step.

How teachers can use it

Use before and after the school swimming program. Read prompts aloud and discuss scenarios using the school and venue’s rules. Reinforce instructor directions and supervision arrangements.

Practical classroom approach

  • Explain the purpose and model an example.
  • Allow students to work at a comfortable pace.
  • Use responses to guide conversation, not as high-stakes assessment.

Guidance for parents and carers

Check the school’s program note for equipment and arrangements. Talk about what your child can do if unsure, cold, tired or needing help. Share concerns with the school.

Keep it supportive

Focus on listening, encouragement and the child’s own words. There is no need to complete every prompt in one sitting.

Inclusive ways to adapt the activities

Use visuals and simple prompts. Accept pointing, drawing or verbal responses. A completed worksheet is not evidence of swimming ability.

Helpful adjustments

  • Offer short sessions and one-step instructions.
  • Accept drawing, speaking, pointing or adult scribing where appropriate.
  • Provide extra processing time and breaks.

Important water-safety reminder

This printable supports preparation and reflection only. It does not teach swimming, assess water competence or replace active, attentive adult supervision, qualified swimming instruction, school risk-management procedures or the swimming venue’s safety rules. Children must be supervised by responsible adults whenever they are in or around water.

Helpful information

Frequently asked questions

Does this pack teach children to swim?

No. It is a preparation and reflection resource. Swimming skills must be taught by appropriately qualified instructors.

Does completing it make a child water-safe?

No. A worksheet cannot assess swimming ability or prevent drowning. Active, attentive adult supervision and venue safety rules are essential.
