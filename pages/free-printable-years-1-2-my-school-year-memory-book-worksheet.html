Purpose of this resource

Support age-appropriate reflection on learning, school experiences and personal growth.

What’s included

  • Choose a favourite school memory
  • think about something learned
  • recognise people who helped
  • draw or write a message for the next teacher
  • set a small goal.

How teachers can use it

Model a simple example. Allow drawing, labels, dictated responses or short sentences. Use as a calm end-of-year activity or portfolio reflection. Avoid comparing students.

Practical classroom approach

  • Explain the purpose and model an example.
  • Allow students to work at a comfortable pace.
  • Use responses to guide conversation, not as high-stakes assessment.

Guidance for parents and carers

Invite your child to talk about a school moment they enjoyed or something they learned. Record their words if needed and let them decide what to include.

Keep it supportive

Focus on listening, encouragement and the child’s own words. There is no need to complete every prompt in one sitting.

Inclusive ways to adapt the activities

Use picture prompts, word banks, sentence starters and adult scribing. Students may draw, leave prompts blank and choose what they share. Do not pressure disclosure of private family details.

Helpful adjustments

  • Offer short sessions and one-step instructions.
  • Accept drawing, speaking, pointing or adult scribing where appropriate.
  • Provide extra processing time and breaks.

Helpful information

Frequently asked questions

Do students need to write full sentences?

No. Drawing, labels, dictated responses and short phrases can all be appropriate for Years 1–2.

Can families complete it at home?

Yes. A parent or carer can listen, ask gentle prompts and record the child’s own words.
