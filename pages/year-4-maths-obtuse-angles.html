What students will practise

  • Identify angles larger than 90 degrees
  • Distinguish obtuse angles from straight angles
  • Describe angle size using correct vocabulary

Learning intention

Recognise obtuse angles by comparing their size with a right angle and a straight angle.

How to use this worksheet

Compare each angle with a right angle first. If it is wider than a right angle but does not make a straight line, it is obtuse.

Curriculum connection

AC9M4M04 — Estimate and compare angles using angle names including acute, obtuse, straight, reflex and revolution, and recognise their relationship to a right angle.

Recognising Obtuse Angles — Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers for parents, teachers, tutors and homeschool families.

What is an obtuse angle?

An obtuse angle is larger than 90 degrees and smaller than 180 degrees.

Is the worksheet free?

Yes. The printable worksheet can be downloaded directly without a sign-up.

How can I explain obtuse angles to a child?

Compare the angle with the square corner of a page: an obtuse angle opens wider than that corner but does not form a straight line.

What worksheet can we use next?

Try comparing angle types or use the Angles Learning Hub to find more practice.
