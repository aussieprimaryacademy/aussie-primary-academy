My child is starting Foundation, where do I start?

Starting school is a big step for children and families. You do not need to teach everything before the first day. Begin with familiar conversations, play and one short activity when your child is ready. This guide points you to five free printable worksheets and a calm way to use them.

What learning looks like in Foundation

Foundation is a year of building confidence, communication, early literacy and numeracy through explicit teaching, exploration and play. Children begin connecting number names, numerals and quantities up to 20, counting in sequence, noticing patterns and comparing objects. In English, they practise listening, talking about stories, recognising letters and sounds, and creating words and short texts. In Science, they observe familiar plants, animals, materials and how objects move. These are learning areas to develop across the year, not a checklist your child must master before school starts. Children begin with different experiences and progress at different rates.

Read the Australian Curriculum parent information for Foundation. Your child’s teacher and school can explain how learning is sequenced for their class.

Three simple ways to help at home

Small, positive moments are more useful than long practice sessions.

  1. Keep it short and playful. Try 5–10 minutes, then stop while your child is still interested.
  2. Use everyday moments: count fruit, notice letters in your child’s name, sort toys or look closely at a plant together.
  3. Talk about what your child notices. Ask “What can you see?” or “How did you know?” and praise effort rather than speed.

Common parent questions

Quick answers to help you get started without pressure.

Does my child need to know all their letters and numbers before Foundation?

No. Children start school with different experiences and skills. Foundation teaching builds early literacy and numeracy over time. If you have a specific concern, speak with your child’s school.

How long should we spend on a worksheet?

Start with about 5–10 minutes, or less if your child is tired. Choose one activity and finish on a positive note. There is no need to complete all five worksheets at once.

Are these Foundation worksheets free and curriculum-aligned?

Yes. The linked worksheet pages provide free printable PDFs and show their Australian Curriculum v9.0 content codes. They are optional practice resources to support learning, not a replacement for classroom teaching.
