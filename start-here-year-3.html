If your child is starting Year 3, begin with the skills they are learning now rather than trying to cover everything. These five free worksheets offer a balanced first set across Maths, English and Science. Pick one area at a time and use your child’s confidence to guide the next step.

What learning looks like in Year 3

Year 3 sits within ACARA’s Years 3 and 4 learning stage for several areas, and the parent guide describes children becoming more independent and communicating more effectively. In English, students read a wider range of imaginative, informative and persuasive texts, create texts to tell stories, persuade and explain, and practise accurate spelling, punctuation and editing. In Mathematics, they build strategies for addition, subtraction, multiplication and division, work with fractions and money, and solve practical problems involving measurement, time, geometry and data. In Science, they develop understanding of living things and investigate questions about the world. Schools sequence this learning across the year and respond to student progress, so use these ideas as a guide rather than a pass-or-fail checklist.

Read the Australian Curriculum parent information for Years 3 and 4. Your child’s teacher and school can explain how learning is sequenced for their class.

Three simple ways to help at home

Small, positive moments are more useful than long practice sessions.

  1. Ask your child to explain how they worked something out. The strategy and reasoning matter as much as the final answer.
  2. Read together and pause to discuss clues, unfamiliar words, characters and the main idea. Let your child point to evidence in the text.
  3. Connect practice to daily life: make equal groups at dinner, notice prefixes in books, or talk about how a plant or animal changes as it grows.

Common parent questions

Quick answers to help you get started without pressure.

Should my child complete all five worksheets before school starts?

No. These are optional starter resources, not a holiday checklist. Choose one activity that matches a skill your child is ready to practise, and leave the others for later.

What if a worksheet feels too easy or too difficult?

If it feels too easy, ask your child to explain their strategy or create another example. If it feels too difficult, work through one example together or choose an earlier skill. Your child’s teacher can help identify the best next step.

Are these Year 3 worksheets free and aligned with the Australian Curriculum?

Yes. The linked worksheet pages provide free printable PDFs and display their Australian Curriculum v9.0 content codes. They are supplementary practice resources for home, classroom, tutoring or homeschool use.
