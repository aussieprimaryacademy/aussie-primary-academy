What are CVC Words? A Simple Guide for Parents (With Free Worksheets)

Is your child in Foundation or Year 1 and starting to read 3-letter words like cat, dog and sun? Those are CVC words — the most important first step to reading.

In this guide, we’ll explain what CVC words are in simple terms, share a clear CVC words list, and show you how to practise them at home in just 5 minutes a day.

What Are CVC Words?

CVC stands for Consonant – Vowel – Consonant.

It’s a simple 3-letter word pattern where each letter makes its most common sound. This makes it easy for early readers to sound out.

CVC Words List and Examples

  • -at family: cat, hat, mat, sat, bat
  • -og family: dog, log, fog, jog
  • -in family: pin, bin, tin, win
  • -en family: pen, hen, ten, men
  • -un family: sun, bun, run, fun
  • -ug family: mug, bug, hug, jug

All these words are perfect for Foundation and Year 1 phonics practice.

Why Are CVC Words So Important?

Before children can read books, they need to learn two skills, and CVC words help with both:

1. Blending: Putting sounds together. c – a – t = cat

2. Segmenting: Breaking a word apart. cat = c – a – t

When your child can blend and segment CVC words, they move from knowing letter sounds to actually reading.

How to Practise CVC Words at Home (5-Minute Ideas)

You don’t need to be a teacher. Short, calm practice is best.

1. Oral Blending Game

Say “m-a-p” slowly and ask, “What word is that?”

2. Word Families

Practise one family at a time. Today only -at words, tomorrow -og words.

3. Find the Sound

Ask, “What is the middle sound in dog?” or “What is the first sound in sun?”

What Comes After CVC Words?

Once your child is confident with CVC words, the next step in Year 1 is:

  • Consonant Blends: bl, cl, br, cr, st, sp
  • Consonant Digraphs: sh, ch, th, wh, ck

You can find free blends and digraph worksheets on Aussie Primary Academy.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should my child start CVC words?

Most children start CVC words in Foundation once they know most letter sounds (A–Z). Some start in early Year 1, which is completely normal.

What are 10 easy CVC words for beginners?

cat, dog, sun, pen, hat, big, map, bed, cup, log. Start with these.

My child is struggling with CVC words. What should I do?

Keep practice to 5–10 minutes, use word families (-at, -og), and focus on oral blending first before writing. Our free CVC worksheets start very simple.

How often should we practise?

Short and regular is best. 5–10 minutes, 3–4 times a week is more effective than one long session.

Final Thought: CVC words are a small step that builds big confidence. With a little patience and the right free resources, your child will go from sounding out c-a-t to reading it proudly on their own.
