Got Your Child's 2026 NAPLAN Results? Here's What to Do Next

Individual NAPLAN reports are landing in family inboxes and school bags across Australia this term. If you've just opened one and aren't quite sure what to make of it, you're not alone — here's a calm, practical way to read it.

What NAPLAN Results Actually Measure

NAPLAN assesses students in Years 3, 5, 7 and 9 across reading, writing, language conventions and numeracy. It's worth repeating something ACARA itself says clearly: NAPLAN is not a pass or fail test.

It's one snapshot, taken on a couple of specific days, of where a child sits against national expectations for their year level. It doesn't measure creativity, effort, curiosity, or the dozens of other things that make a child a capable learner. Think of it less like a report card and more like a single photo — useful information, but not the whole story.

Understanding the Proficiency Levels

Your child's report will describe their result using one of four levels.

  • Exceeding: results well above expectations for the year level
  • Strong: results that meet or comfortably exceed expectations
  • Developing: results showing the skills are still forming, with room to grow
  • Needs Additional Support: results indicating extra support would help going forward

These levels compare your child's results with national benchmarks, not with other children in their class. Two children in "Developing" for different reasons can need completely different kinds of support.

If the Result Was Lower Than You Expected

A result that's lower than expected can feel unsettling, especially the first time — but it isn't a verdict on your child, their teacher, or their school. Testing is one moment in a long, ordinary process of learning, and results move around for all sorts of reasons: an off day, an unfamiliar question style, or simply a skill that's still developing on a normal timeline.

National data for 2026 shows small movements up and down across year levels and subjects — nothing dramatic, in either direction. The most useful response isn't to look for someone or something to blame. It's to treat the result as one data point and ask a simple, forward-looking question: what would help from here?

How to Talk to Your Child About It

Children often take their cues from how the adults around them react. A few things that help:

  • Keep the tone matter-of-fact — NAPLAN is just one test, not a judgement of them
  • Avoid comparing their result to siblings, classmates, or your own school memories
  • Praise effort and curiosity, not just the score
  • If they ask about it, answer honestly but briefly, then move the conversation forward

Practical Next Steps

Small, steady action tends to help more than any single big decision.

1. Talk to the Teacher

Teachers see far more of your child's day-to-day learning than a single test can capture. A short conversation about what the result shows — and whether it matches what they're seeing in class — is usually the most useful next step.

2. Target the Specific Skill, Not "Maths" or "English" in General

If numeracy was the area flagged, look at which part — place value, fractions, or basic operations, for example — rather than treating "maths" as one big problem to fix.

3. Keep Practice Short and Low-Pressure

Two or three 15-minute sessions a week, focused on one skill at a time, tend to work better than long study sessions that build resistance instead of confidence.

4. Remember This Is One Test Among Many Years of Learning

The next NAPLAN test is two years away. There's no need to rush a response — steady, ordinary practice over time matters far more than anything done in the days right after results arrive.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is NAPLAN a pass or fail test?

No. NAPLAN is not a pass or fail test. It is a snapshot of a child's literacy and numeracy skills at one point in time, designed to help schools and parents understand where extra support might help.

What do the NAPLAN proficiency levels mean?

NAPLAN results are reported against four proficiency levels: Exceeding, Strong, Developing, and Needs Additional Support. These describe how a child's results compare with expectations for their year level, not a ranking against other students.

My child's NAPLAN result was lower than expected. Should I be worried?

One result is one snapshot, not the full picture of your child's ability. It's worth a calm conversation with their teacher about what the result shows and what steps, if any, might help — without treating it as a verdict.

How can I support my child after NAPLAN without adding pressure?

Focus on short, low-pressure practice in specific skill areas rather than general study, keep the tone relaxed, and celebrate effort. Free printable worksheets are a simple way to target a specific gap without formal tutoring.

Final Thoughts

However the results landed this year, they're one part of a much longer learning journey — not a final answer about your child. A calm conversation with their teacher, a little targeted practice, and time usually do more good than any single reaction in the days after results arrive.
